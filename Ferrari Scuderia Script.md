**THE OPENING**

Every big carmaker that goes racing has a racing division. Mercedes has one. Ferrari has one. Ferrari's is called Scuderia Ferrari.

Simple.

That part is true. But the story behind that name is backwards.

Ferrari, the car company, built its first car in 1947. Scuderia Ferrari was founded in 1929.

Eighteen years earlier.

The racing team is older than the company it belongs to.

If you follow F1, you might already know this. But some of you are probably wondering: how does a company's racing team come before the company itself?

Why is that?

Welcome to Noosphere. The why behind everything.

To answer it, we need to go back.

**THE BOY**

1908. Near Bologna, Italy. A ten-year-old boy stands in the crowd at a motor race. His father and his brother brought him to watch.

His name is Enzo Ferrari.

It's the first race he's ever seen. Cars roaring past on open roads, faster than anything he's known.

And it gets into his head.

Later, he's reading motoring newspapers. Looking at the photos. The drivers. The cars.

In his memoir, he remembers one summer night in Modena. Mosquitoes everywhere. He's swatting them with one of those motoring newspapers. His best friend asks him: what will you be when you grow up?

Enzo doesn't have to think. He points at a photo on the front page.

"I'll be a racer."

Not "I'll build cars." Not "I'll start a company." A racer.

**THE HORSE**

And he becomes one. 1920, he joins Alfa Romeo as a driver.

1923. He wins a race near Ravenna. After the race, he meets a couple: Count Enrico and Countess Paolina Baracca.

Their son, Francesco, was Italy's top fighter pilot in the First World War. He was killed in 1918. On the side of his plane, he had painted a black horse, standing on its back legs.

His mother tells Enzo: put my son's prancing horse on your cars. It will bring you luck.

Enzo says yes. But he doesn't use it. Not yet.

Hold on to that horse.

**THE DINNER**

By 1929, Enzo isn't only racing Alfas. He's selling them too, as Alfa's dealer for his region.

Autumn 1929. Bologna. The local automobile club holds a dinner to celebrate a new speed record. And the place? The Casa del Fascio, the local headquarters of Italy's ruling party.

Keep that in mind.

Enzo is sitting between two rich men who love racing. Alfredo Caniato, from a textile family. And Mario Tadini, a wealthy amateur driver.

Here's the idea that comes out of that night.

A rich man can buy a race car. That's the easy part. But a race car needs mechanics, a workshop, spare parts, a truck to get it to the track, and someone to handle the entries. For one man, that's a lot.

So why not share it? One team owns the cars, keeps them ready and takes them racing. The gentlemen just turn up and drive.

16 November 1929, it's signed in Modena. Tadini and the Caniato brothers bring the money. Alfa Romeo and Pirelli take shares. Tadini is the chairman.

So you may ask: if Tadini is the chairman, why isn't it called Scuderia Tadini?

Because the money was theirs. Everything else was Enzo's. The cars, the drivers, the contacts at Alfa. He brought the experience.

And his name.

For the first time, the name Ferrari is on something.

And the word they chose? Scuderia. Italian for "stable." A place where horses are kept, trained and raced.

But the horses weren't Enzo's. They were Alfa Romeos.

July 1932. At the 24 Hours of Spa, the stable finally gets its horse. Baracca's black horse goes on the cars, on a yellow shield, the colour of Modena. They finish first and second.

So look at what exists in 1932. A team called Ferrari. A horse badge.

And not a single Ferrari car.

**1933**

Then money runs out. The Depression hits Italy, and Alfa Romeo is in trouble.

1933. On Mussolini's orders, the state takes Alfa over.

Remember where that dinner was held? That same government now owns Alfa Romeo.

And a state-owned company has to watch every Italian lira. Racing burns money. So Alfa pulls its own team out of racing.

But someone still has to race the Alfas. Who?

The stable in Modena.

From 1933, Scuderia Ferrari is basically Alfa Romeo's racing team. Alfa builds the cars. Ferrari races them.

Enzo doesn't own a factory. But his name is on the racing team of one of the biggest car brands in Italy.

**LOSING IT**

It doesn't last.

The German teams, Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union, have their government's money behind them. And they're winning.

So in 1937 Alfa decides: we'll do racing ourselves again. It buys the Scuderia's shares, and at the end of 1937 Scuderia Ferrari is shut down.

Eight years of work. His team. His name. Gone.

Enzo moves to Milan to manage Alfa's new in-house team. An employee again.

That doesn't last either. In 1939 he and Alfa part ways. And the exit deal has a clause: for four years, Enzo can't rebuild his Scuderia.

And he can't put the name Ferrari on a racing car.

Now think about that. The boy who said "I'll be a racer" is now forty-one years old. And he isn't allowed to use his own name.

**THE CAR WITHOUT A NAME**

So what does he do?

He goes back to Modena and starts a company with a name with no Ferrari in it: Auto Avio Costruzioni. On paper, it makes machine tools.

1940. He builds two race cars anyway, for the Mille Miglia road race. One is driven by a young Alberto Ascari.

And the name on the car? Not Ferrari. It's called the 815.

The first car Enzo Ferrari ever built, and his name isn't on it.

It couldn't be.

Then the war comes. His factory moves to a small town called Maranello, works for the war effort, and gets bombed.

By the time the war ends, those four years are long gone.

**1947**

12 March 1947. Maranello. Enzo drives a car out of the factory gate. No bodywork yet. Just an engine and a frame.

It's called the 125 S.

And for the first time, a car carries the name Ferrari. With the black horse, on yellow.

Eighteen years after that dinner, the name is finally on a car of his own.

Team. Horse. Car. All three, finally together.

Now you may ask: is it the same Scuderia from 1929?

Not exactly. That company was shut down in 1937. This is a new company carrying the old name. But the idea never changed: race first.

And look at what kind of car the 125 S is. It isn't a family car or a road car. It's a race car.

Two months later, it wins the Rome Grand Prix.

**THE ROAD CARS**

So where do the road cars come in?

Here's the problem. Before the war, Alfa paid for the cars. Now there's no Alfa. And racing is expensive. Engines, tyres, drivers, travel, a new car every season.

So Enzo sells cars to the public. Rich customers buy them, and the money goes straight back into racing.

The road cars weren't the reason Ferrari existed. They were how the racing got paid for.

**WHERE IS IT NOW**

So where is it now?

Today, Ferrari is a car company, and Scuderia Ferrari is its racing division. It has raced in every Formula One World Championship since the very first one, in 1950. No other team can say that.

And Ferrari isn't the only one with this story. McLaren started as a race team too, and only sold road cars decades later. In Formula One, racing first is actually common.
